Abstract
AI-generated satellite images have emerged as a new vector for disinformation, undermining the once-trusted status of overhead imagery. Advanced generative models can produce highly realistic fake satellite photos, enabling malicious actors to spread false “evidence” of events or conditions on the ground. This paper addresses the threat of falsified satellite imagery by introducing a novel dataset of real, synthetic, and partially manipulated overhead images and proposing a robust forensic detection model. The dataset, created using diffusion models with controllable map-based conditioning, provides ground truth labels and manipulation masks to support supervised learning for both image-level classification and pixel-level forgery localization. On this foundation, we develop a Vision Transformer (ViT) based forensic model with a DINOv3 backbone and an edge-gated segmentation decoder. The model jointly classifies an image as pristine, partially manipulated, or fully synthetic, and simultaneously segments manipulated regions. We detail the model architecture, training losses to improve detection of AI-generated satellite disinformation. The proposed approach represents a step toward credible defenses against geospatial deepfakes, enabling accurate identification and localization of manipulated satellite imagery.
